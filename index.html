Safeguarding human‑AI interaction.

I study how AI systems and online platforms intervene when conversations turn harmful or contested, and how people respond: whether they comply, self‑censor, resist, or change their minds. My work combines large‑scale computational analysis, LLM evaluation, and controlled experiments.

Communal Moderation: How Moderator Type and Message Framing Shape User Responses to Content Removal on Reddit

Siyi Zhou, Lindsay Young, Marlon Twyman, Emilio Ferrara

Analyzes 11.8M moderation events across 9.3M users and 61K subreddits (2021 to 2025). Bot removals drive higher compliance and less self‑censorship than human removals, but for harmful or illegal content they provoke roughly four times more resistance; prosocial, emotionally direct framing works best for severe violations.

Probing LLM Guardrails Across Models, Roles, and Risk Categories

Siyi Zhou, Emilio Ferrara

A cross‑model audit of six commercial LLMs (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Qwen) using a probe dataset that crosses 96 controversial topics in 18 risk categories with five use‑case framings: companion, advisor, content editor, content generator, and information retrieval. Responses are coded with a refusal‑to‑compliance taxonomy using LLM judges validated against human coders.

Safeguarding: AI Refusals vs. Platform Moderation

Siyi Zhou

Compares preventive safeguards in AI (refusal, redirection) with retrospective platform interventions (removal, suspension). Asks which is more persuasive, which backfires, and which is more likely to shift how people see the underlying issue, given that platform moderation signals a community norm while an AI refusal reads as one‑on‑one disagreement.

About

I am a PhD student at the USC Annenberg School for Communication and Journalism, advised by Professor Emilio Ferrara. My research sits at the intersection of computational social science, natural language processing, and human‑AI interaction.

Before Annenberg I earned a master's degree in computer science, and I bring that engineering background to social questions: building data pipelines over hundreds of millions of records on HPC clusters, designing evaluation metrics and LLM‑as‑judge pipelines, and running randomized experiments with human participants.

The thread that ties my work together is a practical question: when a system steps in to keep a conversation safe, what actually works, for whom, and at what cost?
